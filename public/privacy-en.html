Privacy Policy

Japanese Vocabulary Cards · Last updated: August 21, 2026

Japanese Vocabulary Cards (hereafter "the app") does not collect, store, or sell any personal data that can identify you. This page explains exactly what data the app touches, where it is kept, and the only circumstances under which anything leaves your device.

Where your data is kept

The vocabulary books, cards, review progress, and settings you create are all kept in this device's local browser storage. The app has no account system, does not ask you to register, and never asks for your email address, phone number, or name. Apart from the cases spelled out in the two sections below, and the iCloud Keychain mentioned in the cloud backup section, your data does not leave this device.

Cloud backup (optional, off by default)

Cloud backup carries your progress between your own devices. It stays off until you turn it on; while it is off, the app sends no backup request at all.

  • End-to-end encryption. Your data is already encrypted on your device (AES-GCM, an industry-standard symmetric encryption method). The encryption key is derived from your password on the spot with PBKDF2 (Password-Based Key Derivation Function 2, the standard algorithm for deriving a key from a password), exists only in your device's memory, and is never uploaded.
  • Only ciphertext on the server. Backups are stored in Google Firebase Realtime Database. The server holds only the encrypted content, two verification values derived from your password that cannot be reversed back into it (used to confirm that whoever overwrites a backup holds the right password), and an update timestamp. The storage location is labeled with a hash of your nickname, and the nickname itself is never sent in the clear. Neither the server nor whoever administers it can read what you backed up.
  • Your password stays on your device. So that the app can carry on backing up the next time you open it, the nickname and password you type are kept on this device. How they are kept depends on the platform:
    • The web version keeps them in the browser's storage, in plain text. Anyone who can read that storage can equally read every card and all the review progress on this device.
    • The iOS version keeps them in the system Keychain, held encrypted by the system. If you have iCloud Keychain turned on, they sync along with the keychain to your other Apple devices — that is Apple's end-to-end encrypted service, and Apple itself cannot read the contents. If you do not, they stay on this device only.
    In either case, they never go into this app's cloud backup, and they do not appear in the backup file you export.
  • No account, no contact details. You pick the nickname and the password yourself. They serve only to recognize "this is the same person's backup", they correspond to no real identity, and we do not match them against any other data.
A lost password cannot be recovered. Precisely because the encryption key comes only from your password and never leaves your device, nobody — the app's developer included — holds a spare key. Once the password is forgotten, that cloud backup can never be decrypted again, and it cannot be reset.

Gemini reading prefill

While you edit a card, the app can fill in the kana reading of the kanji for you. This feature works in an entirely different way on the web version and in the iOS app, so the two are described separately below.

Web version (optional, off by default)

This feature is off by default, and is enabled only once you supply a Google Gemini API (Application Programming Interface) key of your own.

  • Nothing happens without a key. As long as you have not entered a key, the app sends no request to Google for readings, and the reading cell is always left blank for you to fill in yourself.
  • The key stays on this device. The key is kept on its own in local storage. It does not go into the cloud backup, and it does not appear in the backup file you export. On another device you have to set it up again.
  • Only that one entry is sent. Once enabled, the app sends the Japanese entry you are editing, together with a fixed set of instructions for the task, to Google's Gemini API in exchange for the reading. What is sent stops there: it does not include your meanings, your review progress, or any other card.
  • No third-party server in between. The request goes straight from your device to Google's Gemini API, with no server of ours and no relay service handling it along the way. It uses the key you applied for and your own quota, and that request is governed by Google's privacy policy.

iOS app (on by default)

In the iOS app this feature is on by default. There is nothing for you to set up and no key field on screen — you finish typing the entry and the reading fills itself in. The key is provided by the app's maintainer and is not inside the app.

  • Only that one entry is sent. What goes out is the Japanese entry you are editing, together with a set of instructions for the task chosen by the maintainer. What is sent stops there: it does not include your meanings, your review progress, or any other card.
  • The request does not go straight to Gemini. It first goes to the Firebase project the maintainer keeps with Google (Firebase AI Logic), the key is added there, and it is then handed to Google's Gemini. The maintainer runs no server of their own; this leg happens entirely on Google's machines. It uses the key the maintainer supplies and the maintainer's quota, and that request is governed by Google's privacy policy.
  • Google may use this content to improve its models. The maintainer is on Gemini's free usage tier, and under Google's terms content sent on the free tier may be used by Google to improve its models. That is a decision the maintainer made on your behalf. On the web version you apply for the key yourself and accept Google's terms yourself; in the iOS app that step does not exist — you add a card, you finish typing an entry, and the key already in use is the maintainer's. It is written here so that you know about it.
  • A device attestation is attached to the request. To confirm that the request really comes from this app and not from someone misusing the maintainer's key, the request carries an attestation issued for this device by Apple's App Attest, and one attestation lasts for a while before a new one is needed. That attestation contains nothing that identifies you, and it is not used to track you.
  • An installation identifier is registered with Google. The app uses Firebase Remote Config to decide which model the reading prefill should use and which instructions to send, so that the day Google retires a model the fix does not have to go through App Store review. When it fetches that configuration, Firebase creates an installation identifier, keeps it on this device, and registers it with Google. It identifies this installation, not you: it contains nothing that identifies you, reinstalling or switching devices produces a different one, and we do not compare it against any other data.

No analytics collected, no third-party tracking

The app contains no analytics tools, no advertising SDK (Software Development Kit), and no tracking code. We do not record how many times you opened it, how many cards you reviewed, or which button you pressed; we do not use cookies to track you; and we neither share nor sell any data to third parties. Apart from the two features described above, the app sends none of your data anywhere. (The web version is hosted on GitHub Pages, so loading the page — exactly as with any website you visit — lets that host see your IP (Internet Protocol) address; we neither touch nor keep records of that kind.)

Deleting your data

The data on this device is entirely yours to control: clear this site's browser storage, or remove the app from the device, and the data goes with it. Press "Stop backing up" on the "Data" screen and this device no longer backs up to the cloud, while the cards and progress on the device stay intact. The nickname and password on the iOS version are the only exception: they live in the system Keychain, and removing the app does not clear them — pressing "Stop backing up" does. If you have iCloud Keychain turned on, that copy is removed from the keychain at the same time. The app currently offers no button for deleting the backup already in the cloud, and that ciphertext stays on the server; since only your password can decrypt it, we have no way to read it and will not use it for anything.
